# IRL-001 — Low-Cost Materials Degradation Research

**Research area:** Materials science / corrosion  
**Status:** Research design — experiment not yet performed  
**Project type:** Exploratory, low-cost, image-based study  
**Research lab:** Industrial Research Lab Togo

## Research question
How do different exposure conditions (dry ambient control, freshwater, and saline water; continuous wetting versus wet/dry cycles) influence the visible development of corrosion on nominally uncoated carbon-steel nails?

A secondary methods question is whether a standardized smartphone-photography workflow can document and estimate changes in visible rust-covered area over time.

## Why this matters
Corrosion monitoring is important for the safety, reliability, and maintenance of metallic infrastructure and equipment. Commercial monitoring instruments can be expensive or technically demanding. This pilot explores a narrower and more accessible question: what can be measured transparently with basic materials, a smartphone, and open-source image-analysis software?

This is an educational exploratory study. It is **not** a validated industrial corrosion-rate measurement system.

## Current study design
- 15 nominally uncoated carbon-steel nails, ideally from one package and batch.
- Five exposure groups, three specimens per group.
- Planned observation period: 14 days.
- Standardized photographs at baseline and predefined observation times.
- Visible rust area estimated using ImageJ or Fiji.
- Raw observations, images, deviations, and missing data retained.

See [protocol.md](protocol.md) for the procedure and [data-template.csv](data-template.csv) for the recording sheet.

## Important limitations
1. Nails are not standardized corrosion coupons. Their alloy, surface finish, geometry, and any invisible coating may vary.
2. Visible rust area is not the same as metal loss, corrosion penetration, or corrosion rate.
3. The groups differ in moisture exposure and oxygen availability; results must be interpreted as comparisons of these specific setups, not universal effects of salt or humidity.
4. Three specimens per group provide limited evidence. Report descriptive results and individual variation; do not claim statistical significance.
5. Ambient temperature and relative humidity must be measured if reported. Never invent environmental values.
6. No experimental results are available until the protocol has actually been run and the images analyzed.

## Planned future direction
If the pilot demonstrates a repeatable documentation method, a later phase may investigate a low-cost monitoring concept combining environmental sensing, image capture, and transparent data logging. Electrochemical measurements and predictive models are outside the scope of this first phase.

## Research integrity
- Record the actual date and time of each observation.
- Keep original photographs unchanged.
- Store processed images separately from originals.
- Record every protocol deviation.
- Distinguish measured data, observations, assumptions, and proposed future work.
- Do not present illustrative or simulated data as experimental data.
